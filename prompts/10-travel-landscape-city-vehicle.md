# Travel, landscapes, cities, and vehicles

Ten Qwen Image 3.0 prompt recipes. Replace bracketed inputs before generation. All quoted text is exact; no translation is implied unless explicitly requested. Outputs require human review.

<a id="trv-01"></a>

## TRV-01 — Hill tram travel poster

```text
Create a 3:4 travel-poster concept for a fictional hillside town. Show one small yellow tram climbing a curved street between pastel houses, with the sea visible in the distance. Use original screen-print illustration, warm terracotta shadows and a clear title area. Render only "Take the long way" and "A hillside weekend". Do not copy a real tourism campaign, recognizable landmark or transit logo. Variables: palette and approved title; do not present the scene as a real destination photograph.
```

<a id="trv-02"></a>

## TRV-02 — Walking route illustration

```text
Use [VERIFIED MAP AND ROUTE] to create a 3:4 neighborhood walking guide. Preserve street connections, start and end points, and supplied stops. Highlight one route with a continuous blue line and numbered markers corresponding exactly to the provided list. Use simple architectural icons and legible labels. Do not invent distances, opening hours, accessibility or safe crossing points. Add "Check local conditions" in the footer. Variables: map, route, stops and approved copy.
```

<a id="trv-03"></a>

## TRV-03 — Coastal landscape photograph

```text
Create a 16:9 fictional coastal landscape with layered dark rocks, low sea mist and one narrow path on a grassy headland. Use a natural wide-angle photographic perspective at sunrise, with restrained highlights and believable wave scale. Keep the horizon level and the path physically connected. No people, text, recognizable landmark or impossible sun reflection. Variables: weather, coastal geology and palette; label the result as generated imagery rather than a photograph of an actual destination.
```

<a id="trv-04"></a>

## TRV-04 — Night station scene

```text
Create a 16:9 fictional suburban railway-platform scene after rain. Show one empty bench, a small shelter and a stationary unbranded train in the background. Use realistic mixed warm shelter light and cool sky light, modest reflections and slight atmospheric haze. Keep tracks parallel and shelter supports plausible. No readable schedules, destination signs, people, operator logos or dramatic unsafe activity. Variables: architectural style, weather and lighting.
```

<a id="trv-05"></a>

## TRV-05 — Vehicle identity edit

```text
Use an authorized bicycle photograph to create a square catalog image. Replace only the background with a pale-gray studio and adjust lighting to a soft side source. Preserve frame geometry, wheel size, spokes, drivetrain, accessories, paint and all existing decals exactly. Keep both tires grounded and cast a consistent contact shadow. Do not straighten damaged parts, add components or alter branding. Variables: reference and approved background tone.
```

<a id="trv-06"></a>

## TRV-06 — Cabin retreat illustration

```text
Create a 3:4 fictional mountain-cabin poster with one small timber cabin beside a lake and layered forest silhouettes behind it. Use textured gouache, muted pine green and warm window light. Reserve the upper quarter for "A quieter horizon" and the lower margin for "Cabin study". Keep reflections aligned with the cabin and terrain believable. No real property name, booking claim, star rating or invented amenities. Variables: landscape, palette and approved copy.
```

<a id="trv-07"></a>

## TRV-07 — City transport comparison

```text
Create a 16:9 editorial illustration comparing a bicycle, tram and walking route within one fictional street block. Keep all three modes clearly separated and give each equal visual weight. Use a clean isometric view, muted colors and labels "Cycle", "Ride", and "Walk". Do not add travel times, emissions figures or claims of safety superiority. Keep tracks, sidewalks and crossings connected. Variables: approved street layout and labels.
```

<a id="trv-08"></a>

## TRV-08 — Car four-view board

```text
Use authorized vehicle references to create a 16:9 four-view concept board: front, rear, left side and three-quarter view. Preserve body proportions, wheelbase, lights, mirrors and paint across views. Use the same neutral studio environment and consistent scale. Label each view once; omit number plates or use an explicitly fictional blank plate. No invented badges, range figures or performance claims. Variables: references and exact view labels.
```

<a id="trv-09"></a>

## TRV-09 — Travel journal spread

```text
Create a 3:2 illustrated travel-journal spread for a fictional riverside weekend. Place a bridge sketch on the left and three small studies of a boat, café cup and leafy branch on the right. Use pencil lines, watercolor washes and ivory paper. Render only "River weekend" and "Notes from a slow day"; leave the remaining note areas genuinely blank. No fake tickets, passport stamps, addresses or pseudo-handwriting. Variables: location concept and palette.
```

<a id="trv-10"></a>

## TRV-10 — Weather continuity triptych

```text
Create a 16:9 three-panel view of the same fictional lakeside pier in clear weather, light rain and fog. Lock camera, pier boards, railing, moored boat and shoreline. Change only visibility, sky, wetness and light response. Label panels "Clear", "Rain", and "Fog". Keep reflections physically coherent and do not add or remove objects between panels. No forecast, date or real-location claims. Variables: approved scene reference and weather labels.
```
